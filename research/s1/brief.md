# Study 01 · Brief

The study began from a conversation the site owner had with ChatGPT on
11 September 2026. The exchange is transcribed here because it fixed the
question and the tests before any frame was looked at. Spelling is as
written.

## The claim

> One of the things I see in the charlie kirk videos - are very suspicious
> movements of a couple of security team milliseconds just prior to the
> killing. Both look like they press something and try to hide that they are
> pressing something. One pretends he is scratching his upper left arm.
> Another squeezes something under his armpit with arms folded. Is there
> some convincig analysis you can do on the video that can shed light on
> this.

## The proposed tests (ChatGPT's reply, abridged)

| Test | What it could establish |
| --- | --- |
| Frame-by-frame timing | When each hand or arm begins moving, when any apparent pressing action occurs, and how those moments relate to the first visible impact on Kirk. |
| Multiple camera angles | Whether another view reveals a hand, object, clothing adjustment, or obstruction that changes the interpretation. |
| Visible mechanics | Whether the fingers visibly contact and depress something, or whether the footage only shows an arm tightening. These are different evidentiary claims. |
| Earlier behavior | How often those same individuals make comparable movements during the preceding minutes. A repeated habitual gesture carries different weight from a unique action. |
| Blinded comparison | Whether reviewers shown randomized clips, without the shooting or suggestive captions, consistently identify the same movement as operating an object. |

Points carried into the method:

- At a genuine, constant 30 fps successive frames are about 33 ms apart; at
  60 fps about 17 ms. Slow playback does not add observations between them.
  Duplicated frames, edits and altered playback speed must be checked.
  Professional guidance (SWGDE frame-timing guidance, v1.1) prefers original
  recordings and cautions against timing analysis from transcoded or
  screen-recorded copies.
- The audible bang cannot serve as the exact instant of firing: sound travel
  and audio/video synchronization matter. A movement before the recorded bang
  could still occur after the shot was fired. A proposed "signal to shoot"
  would need time for the signal, the shooter's response and the projectile's
  travel.
- Three conclusions must be kept apart: an observable movement; an
  identifiable action; a purpose. Even a clearly established button press
  would leave the device's function unresolved.
- Two movements close to the killing become more compelling if they are
  distinctive, rare and independently confirmed; they carry less weight if
  similar gestures recur throughout the event. The two people could also be
  responding to the same ordinary cue.
