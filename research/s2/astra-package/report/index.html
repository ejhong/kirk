01 / THE SLEEVE CONTACT

The pre-event contact is visible in the clearer source

In 2.MOV, the right hand becomes visible rising toward the left upper-arm/sleeve area around 8.11–8.16 s. Contact is visible around 8.19–8.24 s. Kirk’s abrupt movement is around 8.35 s.

That places the contact roughly 0.1–0.2 seconds before the visual reference. The hand remains in the area through the event and into later frames, with the fingers repositioning.

The hand movement therefore cannot simply be a response to Kirk’s already visible reaction. Its purpose remains unresolved: the frames show contact with the sleeve area, but no identifiable actuator and no visible change in a control’s state.

Assessment: confirmed pre-event contact; function undetermined. Neither “covert activation” nor “definitely an innocent scratch” is established by these pixels.

Muted, approximately 4× slower. Every selected source frame is displayed once in sequence. No generated intermediate frames.

02 / THE BLUE-SHIRTED MAN

The clear unfolding occurs afterward

His arms are already folded before the event. The relevant hand is substantially hidden by the arm and torso. The footage does not resolve a distinct pre-event squeeze of an identifiable object.

In kirk2.mp4, the visual reference is around 6.31 s. Clear unfolding and hand emergence occur around 6.84–6.94 s, roughly 0.5–0.6 seconds afterward. The hand lowers toward the front/waist around 7.10–7.30 s.

A small bright detail is visible as the hand emerges, but it cannot be identified reliably as a device. There is no sufficiently clear, continuous view of an object moving into a pocket or another storage location.

Assessment: folded posture and post-event unfolding are visible. A hidden squeeze and device stowing remain unverified. This is not a finding that the hand is empty.

The review follows the hand through the event. Enlargement cannot recover detail hidden by an arm or absent from the source.

03 / WHAT 7.MP4 ADDS

The hand-under-arm position appears much earlier

The close portion of 7.mp4 shows bent fingers beneath the folded upper arm around 3.6–4.6 s. The visible event occurs around 25.37 s in that file. The posture is therefore present roughly 21 seconds earlier.

This makes the posture itself less distinctive to the final moment. It does not establish what, if anything, is inside the obscured palm. By the shooting, the camera has zoomed out and the relevant hand is too small or occluded to resolve the alleged actuation.

Overall judgment: these files establish a pre-event sleeve contact and an already existing folded-arm posture. They do not establish two coordinated device activations. No defensible numerical probability of coordination or involvement follows from this sample.

What is reproducible

The package preserves exact source hashes, decoded frame timestamps, crop coordinates, selected frame indices, and manual annotation windows. The Python script verifies the inputs and rebuilds all three boards, both slowed clips, and this page. The visual judgments are explicitly manual; the script does not automatically detect intent or identify devices.

The source videos are unchanged. The review aids apply metadata rotation, standard decoding to RGB, cropping, and nearest-neighbor scaling. The slowed clips are re-encoded for viewing. HDR conversion and encoder/library versions can change rendered pixel values, so source hashes and frame indices are the primary audit anchors.

Timing and uncertainty

Per-frame presentation timestamps are used instead of assuming every encoded frame interval is identical. For example, kirk2.mp4 has an approximately 204 ms initial interval, outside the event window. File timing is not authenticated camera timing. These observations do not establish the precise instant of firing, audio synchronization, rolling-shutter timing, or the physical duration between movements.

The approach is an exploratory review, not a forensic timing certification. See SWGDE’s frame timing guidance for the distinction between file frame intervals and defensible forensic timing.

What would materially improve the inference

An unobstructed view of the blue-shirted man’s hand at the event, a visible device with an identifiable function, several minutes of earlier behavior, or independent equipment/communications evidence would help distinguish explanations. More magnification of already occluded or unresolved pixels would not supply that evidence.
